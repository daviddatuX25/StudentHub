Research gaps in SUCs — your chapter 1 and 2 backbone
SUC server infrastructure exists but is severely underutilized
Most CHED institutions have procured servers through DBM/CHED grants, but these sit barely touched — locked down because of PAR liability, absence of dedicated sysadmin staff, and fear of audit exposure. Research gap: no published model exists for how a student-led body can legitimately operate edge infrastructure within a SUC without conflicting with government procurement rules.
No low-cost or zero-cost hosting environment for IT student projects
Philippine SUC students lack access to cloud infrastructure for academic deployment — AWS/GCP require credit cards, free tiers are unreliable and internet-dependent, and school servers are inaccessible. No study has proposed a campus-local PaaS model as an alternative to commercial cloud for BSIT capstone projects.
Campus organizations lack digital financial infrastructure
Student org fund collection — dues, fines, crowdfunding — still relies on physical cash boxes and paper ledgers in most SUCs. E-wallet solutions (GCash, Maya) require accounts, face BSP regulation, and exclude unbanked students. No campus-embedded micro-payment API has been documented in Philippine academic literature as a transparent alternative.
Campus internet access is centrally bottlenecked, not student-accessible
CHED SUCs receive internet through DICT's freeWi-Fi program or institutional ISP contracts, but bandwidth is centrally controlled, underpowered per student, and inaccessible in non-admin zones. No self-sustaining, revenue-generating access model has been proposed as a complement to institutional internet in Philippine SUC literature.
No replicable SUC-specific edge infrastructure model exists
Global literature on edge computing and campus networking focuses on large universities with dedicated IT departments and significant capex budgets — not on resource-constrained Philippine SUCs. The gap is a deployable, documented, open reference architecture that a student group can stand up, operate, and hand off to the next batch.
Problems and how StudentHub solves them — full picture
Student / IT Learner
No place to host and test real apps
Students have no deployment environment — only localhost. Learning CI/CD, Docker, or microservices is theoretical.
Coolify PaaS on local edge node — zero cost, zero internet dependency
Student / Consumer
Internet access is slow, expensive, and unreliable
School Wi-Fi is bottlenecked. Mobile data costs are real. Students pay per load just to access a quiz or form.
Tiered PisoWiFi access — affordable, local ISP line separate from school network
Student Org / Treasurer
Cash collection is opaque and prone to loss
Org dues, fines, abuloy — all manual. No audit trail, receipts go missing, treasurers get accused.
Payment API with immutable transaction log and digital receipt generation
Campus Events
Voting and ticketing is easy to manipulate
Paper tickets for intramurals and pageants are easy to forge or miscounted.
Coin-triggered cryptographic token generation — one coin, one verifiable vote
Faculty / Admin
Existing campus apps go down with the internet
Library systems, enrollment mirrors, grade portals — dependent on external ISP. Crash during peak periods.
Locally hosted apps on Coolify — 100% uptime regardless of external internet health
Institution / CHED
Server assets are procured but not operationalized
Servers purchased via government funds sit underused due to PAR liability and lack of sysadmin staff.
Student-managed edge node model — no PAR burden, no full-time sysadmin required
Institution / CAS IGP
SUC income-generating projects lack digital channels
Most IGPs are physical (canteen, printing). No campus IGP in Philippine SUC literature uses a networked, self-sustaining digital infrastructure.
PisoWiFi revenue funds the ISP line — IGP that pays for its own infrastructure
Next Batch / Replicability
Campus innovations don't survive beyond their creators
Student-built systems are abandoned after capstone defense. No handoff model, no documentation standard.
Open architecture + policy framework designed explicitly for institutional handoff and SUC replication

Introduction
Background: Digital infrastructure gap in Philippine SUCs — underutilized servers, no student hosting environment, cash-only org finance
Statement of the Problem: The target prospect campus (e.g., ISPSC Tagudin) lacks a self-sustaining local network and hosting environment accessible to students and organizations
Significance: Model for SUC-wide adoption; first documented campus edge + micro-payment system in Philippine BSIT literature
Scope: Target prospect campus (e.g., ISPSC Tagudin) boundary, CAS IGP framework, limited to local network boundary

The replicability angle — why this matters beyond ISPSC
The core claim
StudentHub is not just a system for ISPSC Tagudin — it is a documented reference model that any SUC with a student organization, an available physical space, and an ISP connection can replicate without government procurement, without MIS department involvement, and without cloud budget.
What makes it replicable
All components are open-source (Proxmox, Coolify, openNDS, Laravel). Hardware is commodity (ESP32, generic coin/bill acceptors). Policies are drafted and included as appendices. A new batch can clone the repo and redeploy.
The gap it fills in literature
No published Philippine academic study has proposed a student-operated, self-funding campus edge infrastructure. Most related work focuses on enterprise networking or large-university IT departments. This is the first SUC-specific, IGP-integrated, open-architecture reference.
How to say it in defense
"This project is significant not only as a functioning system for ISPSC Tagudin, but as a replicable blueprint — the first documented model in Philippine SUC literature for a student-managed, self-sustaining campus edge infrastructure that operates without institutional IT dependency."
