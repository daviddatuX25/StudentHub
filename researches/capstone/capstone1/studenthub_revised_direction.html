Two Directions, One System

After reviewing the panel feedback and legal constraints, the capstone direction has been refined into two distinct framings — each with its own title, scope, and defense posture. The system underneath is the same. The story we tell changes.

01

Direction A — Innovation & Smart Campus

Focus: the system as a technical platform — edge compute, IoT payment nodes, local PaaS. Domain is innovation and smart campus. CAS IGP is a possible post-deployment adoption, not a core scope constraint.

"Self-sustaining" captures the PisoWiFi revenue loop without saying "vending." Clean, comprehensive, no IGP framing baked in. Covers the full technical scope.

03

Revised Problems & Solutions

Student · IT Learner
No accessible, zero-cost hosting environment for student projects
Cloud hosting requires credit cards most students don't have. Free tiers are internet-dependent, unreliable during defense, and impose limits that make real-world testing difficult. Students demo on localhost — limiting exposure to deployment, CI/CD, and live system behavior.
Coolify-managed local PaaS — deploy apps, databases, and APIs on-campus with zero external dependency and zero cost
Student · Consumer
Internet access is slow, centrally bottlenecked, and costly per load
DICT freeWi-Fi and institutional ISPs are shared across the entire campus — bandwidth per student is thin and unreliable. Mobile data is an out-of-pocket cost. Students pay to access a quiz, submit a form, or attend a class — basic academic participation carries a price tag.
Tiered PisoWiFi access node — dedicated ISP line, affordable per-session pricing, self-sustaining via its own revenue
Org · Treasurer
Campus fund collection is cash-only with no digital alternative
Student org dues, event fees, fines, and abuloy are collected via physical envelopes with no audit trail. E-wallets require BSP compliance — not viable for informal campus use. Paper receipts are lost, ledgers are disputed, treasurers are accused.
Payment API — a technical layer for developers to build collection apps against; adoption by org or SUC is a governance decision, not a system constraint
Innovation · Developer
Campus problems that IT students can solve have no deployment home
Queue management, canteen ordering, event registration, document tracking, voting, org dashboards — none get built and sustained because there is nowhere to host them, no payment layer to connect them to, and no audience if external internet goes down. The bottleneck is not talent. It is infrastructure.
Coolify PaaS + open Payment API — a developer ecosystem for campus-specific apps across all courses and departments
Cross-Department · Multi-Course
IT systems are siloed within the IT department
Technology in SUCs is typically confined to BSIT capstones — other courses have no access to a deployment environment. A BSED student's research data, an Agriculture student's environmental monitoring, a BSHM student's event management workflow, or a BSBA student's inventory tracker could all benefit from local hosting and connected IoT sensors.
An open local platform reachable by any department — IT builds the infrastructure, all courses can deploy on top of it
Hardware · Entry Barrier
Edge infrastructure is perceived as expensive and inaccessible to students
A functional StudentHub node can start with a single mid-range mini PC running Proxmox — scalable from a ₱8,000 entry point. The architecture is intentionally modular: start with one node, one coin acceptor, one ISP connection. Scale as revenue and demand grow.
Modular, scalable hardware architecture — low entry cost, expands as campus adoption grows, self-funded through IGP revenue
Institution · CHED · Legal
Government procurement rules prevent institutional adoption of student-built infrastructure
RA 9184 makes it difficult to absorb student-built hardware into official inventory without bidding and COA compliance. This is a policy and governance gap — not a system constraint. It belongs in recommendations, not in scope.
Addressed in research recommendations — adoption pathways, SUC policy frameworks, and IGP formalization as post-deployment steps
Institution · Sustainability
Student-built systems rarely survive beyond their creators
No handoff culture, no documentation standard, no incentive for the next batch to maintain what they didn't build. StudentHub needs to be designed from the start as a handoff-ready, batch-transferable system — with open-source components, documented policies, and a deployment guide a new team can follow without the original developers.
Addressed in future work — open architecture, deployment documentation, and SUC replication guide as research outputs

Chapter 1 Anchors

Side-by-side comparison of how each direction frames the Chapter 1 introduction — background, problem, significance, scope, and limitations.

Direction A — platform / innovation
Chapter 1
Introduction
Background: The gap between campus problems IT students can solve and the infrastructure needed to deploy solutions — no hosting, no payment layer, no local network independence
Problem: The target prospect campus (e.g., ISPSC Tagudin) lacks a self-sustaining local digital infrastructure for student app deployment, affordable internet access, and campus-level micro-payment processing
Significance: Enables a campus developer ecosystem; fosters cross-department tech utilization; demonstrates a self-funding infrastructure model
Scope: Physical boundary of target prospect campus (e.g., ISPSC Tagudin); local network only; CAS IGP adoption is out of scope — treated as a post-deployment recommendation
Limitations: System operates within local network — external internet access for hosted apps is not covered; formal institutional adoption is not within the study period
Direction B — SUC gap study
Chapter 1
Introduction
Background: Philippine SUCs are underserved by digital infrastructure — underutilized servers, no student hosting environment, cash-only org finance, internet bottlenecks — and no replicable model exists to address this
Problem: There is no documented, deployable reference model for a student-operated, self-sustaining campus edge infrastructure in Philippine state universities
Significance: This study produces not just a working system but a replicable model — applicable to other SUCs, BSIT systems projects, and future research
Scope: Target prospect campus (e.g., ISPSC Tagudin) as deployment site and case study; generalizability to other Philippine SUCs is addressed in conclusions and recommendations
Limitations: Deployment is limited to one campus site; institutional policy adoption is outside the research scope and addressed as a recommendation
